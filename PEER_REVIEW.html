The one thing all four reviewers said

The paper's real contribution is a measurement correction — a biology confound inflates membership-inference attacks on gene-expression models — but it is packaged as a broad unlearning-failure verdict that the evidence does not carry.

By the paper's own retrain-versus-baseline logic, four of the five cohorts (Tabula Muris, TCGA-BRCA, PBMC cluster 7, and the newly added PBMC cluster 12) show absence of a membership signal, not failure of unlearning. The strong claim — "no post-hoc method reaches retrain-level privacy" — is demonstrated on essentially one forget set: PBMC cluster 13, 30 megakaryocytes. Three reviewers reached this independently; the fourth reached it from the theory side. The fix is reframing, not new experiments: lead with the confound result and scope the failure claim to the single cohort that carries a signal.

What is working

A publishable core, and honest reporting underneath it.

  • The biology-confound correction is genuinely valuable and well-instrumented. That the TCGA-BRCA retrain model detects Basal patients better than the baseline (0.860 vs 0.821), and that within-subtype matching collapses the gap, cleanly reframes recent transcriptomics MIA benchmarks as biological fidelity rather than memorization. (all four)
  • The numbers trace and nothing is fabricated. R2 and R3 independently checked the main table, attack suite, and Fisher values against the canonical JSON outputs and found them faithful. Proposition 1's factorization is algebraically correct and its rank-1 prediction holds (94–98% of Frobenius norm). (R2, R3)
  • Broad, mechanism-level method coverage. Nine post-hoc methods plus DP-SGD plus two constructive approaches, each failure attributed to a named mechanism (posterior collapse, Streisand, encoder-signal survival) rather than a bare leaderboard. (R1, R2)
  • Unusually candid statistics. The permutation-null floor, the folded per-seed estimator, and the disclosure that the retrain reference sits only marginally above chance are rigor most unlearning papers omit. (R2, R4)

Issues, ranked by severity and consensus

Fifteen findings. None fatal, none requiring new core experiments — but the top tier blocks acceptance as framed.

Tier 1 — Claims & framingblocks acceptance as written
1A confound result is sold as a broad unlearning-failure verdict BlockerR1R2R4

The title, Contribution 2, and the abstract's closing line ("If privacy actually matters, retrain") read as a general negative result on unlearning. The genuine-signal test reduces to one forget set (cluster 13, n=30).

Fix Re-title and re-abstract around the measurement/confound contribution; scope "no post-hoc method reaches retrain-level privacy" to PBMC-33k explicitly wherever it appears.

2"Eleven methods across three datasets" overstates the grid MajorR1R2R4

The full suite runs on PBMC only. Tabula Muris gets extra-gradient + Fisher; TCGA-BRCA gets six methods. The systematic-evaluation claim is not honestly scoped.

Fix State that the eleven-method suite is PBMC-only and that TM/TCGA receive method subsets.

3Extra-gradient's verdict is estimator-dependent and incoherent on cluster 12 BlockerR2R3R4

Extra-gradient is the only method that materially reduces the signal, and its FAIL label flips with the estimator (folded per-seed 0.281 → FAIL; advantage-of-pooled-AUC 0.134 → PASS; diff-from-retrain CI includes zero). On cluster 12 it reaches AUC 0.497 (chance), which passes the paper's own success rule (advantage ≤ 0.64), yet is labeled "over-unlearn / FAIL." Sections 5 and 6 also disagree on the value (0.03 vs 0.00).

Fix Rest the verdict on variance/reliability (σAUC 0.142, per-seed AUC 0.24–0.66), not the point estimate. Report the cluster-12 AUC, reconcile 0.00 vs 0.03, and reframe cluster 12 as hyperparameter brittleness, not signal-removal failure.

Tier 2 — Theory overreachthe explanation claims more than it proves
4The paper's own experiment relocates the cause to the encoder BlockerR3

The headline mechanism is the shared decoder output (0.485 vs a classifier's 0.018). But the cluster-conditional experiment orthogonalizes exactly that layer (cosine 5.5e-9) and unlearning still fails — because the MIA reads 64 of 70 features from the encoder. The advertised locus is not where the attack signal lives.

Fix Reframe the structural argument around encoder/bottleneck overlap as the binding constraint, and add the encoder-side comparison that is the correct control.

5"High overlap ⇒ must fail" is necessary-not-sufficient, and the scaling is overstated MajorR3

A cosine of 0.485 is a 61° angle — closer to orthogonal than parallel — leaving a large subspace to perturb the forget set alone; no threshold separates "fail" (0.485) from "succeed" (0.018). And full overlap = cos(σ)·cos(ν) saturates at cos(ν) ≈ 0.60, not 1, so the abstract's "grows as 1 − O(M/D)" describes only one of two factors.

Fix Present the theory as an explanation for observed alignment, not a proof of impossibility; correct the "grows to 1" language to the ≈0.60 saturation.

6The theory covers 3 of 9 methods MajorR3

The Fisher-overlap mechanism bears only on the diagonal-Fisher methods (Fisher, SSD, SCRUB). Extra-gradient — the one method not separable from retrain — is entirely outside its scope, yet the abstract states Fisher overlap as the blanket cause.

Fix Narrow the causal claim to Fisher-based methods, or supply a separate argument for the others.

7The Cheng (2026) rebuttal is untested and uses the wrong quantity MajorR1R3

Cheng's Wasserstein-2 projection acts on mean-gradient directions, but the rebuttal applies the diagonal-Fisher (second-moment) cosine of 0.485 — a category slip — and the method is never run on the VAE. A rebuttal-by-prediction invites "then run it."

Fix Label it a conjecture and report the mean-gradient cosine, or instantiate Cheng on the PBMC VAE.

Tier 3 — Traceability & validitynumbers that don't reconcile
8The flagship within-subtype control is flagged invalid by the authors' own tool BlockerR2

confound_fraction_cis.json marks the PBMC c13 decomposition valid: false (small_n, CI [−0.373, 0.985]) — it rests on 5 unseen megakaryocytes. And the within-cluster numbers don't trace: the text uses baseline 0.527 / retrain 0.609, but matched_negative_validation.json gives 0.596 / 0.653.

Fix Reconcile the two files, disclose the invalidity, and rest the corroboration on cluster 7 (80 negatives), not c13's 5.

9Several secondary headline numbers don't match the canonical JSON MajorR2

TCGA cross-subtype is 0.821 / 0.860 in text vs 0.802 / 0.830 in unlearning_results.json; the cluster-12 baseline is 0.75 in Section 5 vs 0.731 in the summary; baseline drifts across 0.769 / 0.781 / 0.791 and retrain across 0.481 / 0.513 / 0.578 by section.

Fix Reconcile every in-text number to one canonical file; standardize the main narrative on the multi-seed regime and confine single-seed values to a labeled appendix.

10Cluster-12 vs cluster-13 comparison mixes units FixR4

"Genuine gap ≈ 0.11 … less than cluster 13's 0.21" compares an advantage gap (0.11) against an AUC gap (0.21). In advantage units cluster 13's gap is ≈ 0.42.

Fix Restate both gaps in the same metric. (Introduced in this session's cluster-12 edit.)

11"Retrain ≥ baseline ⇒ no memorization" may reflect a saturated attacker MajorR2

On TCGA within-subtype, baseline and retrain AUC are identical to 15 significant figures — two different models producing a byte-identical score suggests the attacker cannot discriminate at n=30, not that leakage is provably zero.

Fix Refit a dedicated attacker on the retrain model to show even a targeted adversary finds nothing.

Tier 4 — Reproducibility, positioning & clarityquick to fix, worth fixing
12No reproducibility statement; determinism caveat omitted MajorR2R4

No environment, no split seed, no note that scoring must be deterministic (CPU) because MPS is not bit-reproducible, and no definition of the advantage estimator that changes the extra-gradient number (0.281 vs 0.134).

Fix Add a Reproducibility appendix with environment, the CPU-scoring requirement, seeds, and the estimator definition.

13Generalization to non-VAE generative models is asserted, VAE-only FixR1R3

Every experiment uses one scVI-style VAE; "extends to other generative models with shared output parameters" is a conjecture.

Fix Hedge consistently, or add one non-VAE decoder to substantiate the structural claim.

14Related-work gaps FixR1

Directly relevant entries sit in the bib but are uncited (Pawelczyk 2025 on unlearning failing to remove poisoning; Basu 2021 on influence-function fragility); generative-unlearning and named distance-attack citations are missing.

Fix Cite the uncited-but-relevant entries; add 2–3 generative-unlearning references; attribute the distance attacks.

15Clarity: undefined terms, prose colons, section ordering FixR4

Undefined "Eff. rank ratio (F)" column and TTUR / ARI acronyms; three prose colons against the authors' own convention; cluster-12 unlearning results sit in the confound section but forward-reference Table 4 in the results section.

Fix Define or drop the metric, expand acronyms, remove prose colons, move cluster-12 results into Section 6.

The four reviews in full

Each reviewer's summary and headline points.

R1 Novelty, contribution & positioning ›

Summary. The bare negative result ("approximate unlearning does not match retraining") is not new, but the combination of the confound correction, the classifier-vs-generative Fisher contrast with a closed-form scaling law, and the transcriptomics domain is a genuine if moderate contribution.

Top points. The three-dataset framing oversells evidence that reduces to one dataset. The generative-model generality rests entirely on scVI-style VAEs. The Cheng rebuttal is a prediction about a method that is never run — Moon, by contrast, is run and fails for a concrete reason. Three directly relevant references (Pawelczyk 2025, Basu 2021, Goldman 2020) sit uncited in the bib.

R2 Experimental rigor & claim support ›

Summary. Most core numbers trace cleanly and the confound finding is well-instrumented, but the "unlearning fails" headline is supported, in the strong sense, by essentially a single forget set, and several secondary numbers do not trace to the canonical files.

Top points. Four of five additional cohorts fall in the "no signal to unlearn" bucket. The cluster-12 "FAIL" contradicts the paper's own success criterion (extra-gradient reaches chance AUC 0.497). The within-subtype PBMC control is flagged valid: false by the authors' own tool. TCGA and within-cluster numbers don't match the primary JSON. The extra-gradient verdict flips with the estimator; rest it on variance instead.

R3 Theoretical correctness ›

Summary. The algebra is sound — Proposition 1's factorization is correct and its rank-1 prediction is verified — but the inferential chain from "Fisher directions are aligned" to "no method can selectively unlearn" is necessary-not-sufficient and, in one place, contradicted by the paper's own experiment.

Top points. The cluster-conditional experiment orthogonalizes the decoder output yet still fails because encoder overlap dominates, relocating the causal mechanism away from the advertised layer. A cosine of 0.485 is a 61° angle with no established threshold; full overlap saturates at ≈0.60, not 1. The theory covers only the three diagonal-Fisher methods. The Cheng rebuttal substitutes a second-moment cosine for a first-moment gradient direction.

R4 Clarity, framing honesty & reproducibility ›

Summary. The work is careful and mostly candid about the confound, but the packaging presents a measurement result as a broad unlearning-failure result, and several load-bearing numbers are scattered, unit-mismatched, or reported at two values across sections.

Top points. The headline over-generalizes a confound result. The extra-gradient verdict is contradictory across six locations. The cluster-12 vs cluster-13 comparison mixes advantage and AUC units. There is no reproducibility statement and no determinism caveat despite the CPU-scoring requirement. An undefined "Eff. rank ratio" column and three prose colons violate the authors' own conventions.

What this review is not. No reviewer found fabricated or manipulated data — R2 and R3 verified the numbers against the canonical JSON outputs. Every finding is a matter of framing, scope, theory-overreach, or internal consistency, all addressable by re-analysis and rewriting rather than new experiments. That is why the decision is revision, not rejection, and why the underlying science is regarded as a publishable core for TMLR.

Method, and its bias. Four independent reviewer agents each read the current Writeup.tex and the canonical output JSONs through one assigned lens, with no visibility into each other's conclusions, then were synthesized into this packet. This is a deliberately adversarial internal review meant to pre-empt real reviewers, not a calibrated accept/reject prediction. Two of the four were explicitly pointed at the confound-versus-unlearning framing tension, so the unanimity on that point partly reflects the brief rather than the paper alone; the traceability, theory, and internal-consistency findings were surfaced independently by the reviewers grepping the outputs. Two concrete findings (issues 3 and 10) trace to edits made in the same working session that produced this review, and are flagged as such.
